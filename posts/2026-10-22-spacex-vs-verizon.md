# Betting on SpaceX over Verizon

*October 22, 2026*

I've been watching Starlink creep into the phone business for a while, and I finally put a bet on it: **SpaceX up, Verizon down**, both short term and about the same size on each side. Here's what I'm holding, why, and how it can go wrong.

## The thesis

Starlink isn't just home internet anymore. It's going after your phone.

- **SpaceX bought the spectrum.** In September 2025 SpaceX agreed to buy wireless spectrum licences from EchoStar for about $17 billion, specifically to grow its direct-to-cell business. SpaceX said the upgraded satellites would expand cell capacity by "more than 100 times." ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacex-buys-wireless-spectrum-echostar-17-billion-deal-2025-09-08/))
- **The FCC signed off.** On May 12, 2026 the FCC approved the deal: 65 MHz of nationwide AWS-4, H-block and AWS-3 spectrum going to SpaceX in two steps, with the final transfer expected by about November 30, 2027. ([FCC order](https://www.fcc.gov/document/order-granting-spacex-echostar-applications), [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/fcc-approves-spacex-spectrum-deal-with-2-4-billion-escrow-condition/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/05/fcc-angers-small-carriers-by-helping-att-and-starlink-buy-echostar-spectrum/))
- **And then the satellites.** On October 6, 2026 the FCC approved up to 15,000 direct-to-device satellites for what SpaceX now calls Starlink Mobile. Gwynne Shotwell says the next-gen system could be "100 times better" than today's, with service targeted by the end of next year. ([Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/10/07/fcc-grants-spacex-approval-for-15000-direct-to-device-satellites-in-mss-spectrum/))

To me, that's the spectrum puzzle coming together for SpaceX: its own airwaves, regulatory approval, and a constellation built for phones. Starlink isn't a carrier yet ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/05/fcc-angers-small-carriers-by-helping-att-and-starlink-buy-echostar-spectrum/)), but it's partnered with T-Mobile and Boost, not Verizon. My view: the market will keep rewarding SpaceX for that story and keep worrying about what it means for the big terrestrial carriers. Verizon is a mature, slow-growth carrier, and I think it's the one that feels the pressure.

That's my opinion, not a fact. Verizon has a huge network, and satellite-to-phone service is still small next to it.
